## 1. Worked cost arithmetic per route (round trip at 20 USD and 1,000 USD notional; minimum gross edge to break even)

### Takeaway
At 20 USD, IBKR Ireland's per-order minimums and the 1% fractional/cap rule make a US-stock round trip cost about 1-2% of notional (6-7 R at a 0.3% stop), which no published ORB edge can cover; zero-minimum routes (XTB real GPW shares at 0% commission, index/FX CFDs charging only a spread of roughly 0.005-0.01%) are the only ones where cost per round trip is smaller than the 0.08-0.13 R edge. At 1,000 USD, IBKR Tiered US stocks fall to about 0.08% (0.26 R at a 0.3% stop) and index CFDs to about 0.01%, but futures (about 32,000 USD notional per MES contract) are structurally not sizeable for either account.

Break-even edge expressed in R: cost_R = (round-trip cost % of notional) / (stop distance % of price). At a 0.3% stop: IBKR Fixed at 1,000 USD 0.67 R; IBKR Tiered 0.26 R; Capital stock CFD 0.49 R; XTB stock CFD 1.0 R; index CFD about 0.03 R; at 20 USD IBKR Fixed about 6.7 R. The published Zarattini average edge is +0.08 R (stocks in play, no slippage), so no stock route clears it except XTB real GPW shares at 0% commission (spread and the stop-distance caveat still apply). Index/FX CFD costs in R depend on the stop in points: 2.5 pts round trip in the MQL5 study corresponds to roughly 0.13 R on NQ.

- Fixed vs Tiered at 20 USD is a rounding error: both end up at the 0.5-1% per-side cap, so 20 USD cannot fund a US-stock ORB at IBKR; the account size, not the schedule, dominates [M, from the arithmetic above].
- Futures sizing: 1 MES = 5 USD/point (about 32,500 USD notional), 1 MNQ = 2 USD/point, 1 Micro-DAX = EUR 1/point. A 10-point MES stop is 50 USD, i.e. 5% of a 1,000 USD account (unsolvable at 1% risk), and margin for even one contract is likely several hundred USD or more [M-L; margin unverified].
- CFD sizing: Capital.com minimum 0.001 of US100 and 5% margin means one minimum position needs about 1.25 USD of margin at an index level of 25,000 (level assumed), so a 20 USD account can trade; but profit per trade is cents.
- Overnight financing only matters if ORB positions are carried: Capital.com long US100 about 8.2% p.a. on full notional (0.02237% x 365), equal to 0.45% of posted margin per day at 5% margin; XTB DE40 long about 6.0% p.a. An intraday-only engine should flatten before the funding time (Capital.com 21:00 UTC, XTB midnight).
- Capital.com's 21:00 UTC funding time equals the US cash close in US winter time (16:00 EST = 21:00 UTC), so a close-of-day exit has no slack in winter; use a time stop around 20:55 UTC or earlier [inference; whether Capital.com shifts the funding time with DST was not checked].

## 2. Strategy fit and timing (hours in Warsaw time, DST mismatch, evidence on ORB for index CFDs/futures/EU)

### Takeaway
Index CFDs and index futures are the instrument class where the opening-range edge is measurable but small (gross about +0.10-0.15 R, net about 0 after realistic CFD costs), and the only credible 2026 evidence on indices says net edge is indistinguishable from zero; the project should expect a null result and define success as a verified, reconciled net P&L rather than profit. The US cash session is 15:30-22:00 Warsaw time from 2 Nov 2026 but 14:30-21:00 during US/EU DST-mismatch weeks (25-31 Oct 2026, 8-28 Mar 2026, and about 14-27 Mar 2027), so the ORB schedule must be derived from exchange time zones, not fixed CET offsets.

- Eurex regular trading is 08:00-22:00 CET (European hours 08:00-17:30, "U.S. hours" 17:30-22:00); extended "Asian" hours 01:00 CET (02:00 CEST) to 08:00 for selected liquid futures. — [Eurex trading hours](https://www.eurex.com/ex-en/trade/trading-hours) [H]

### Inferences
Session map in Warsaw time (derived from exchange hours above; US 09:30-16:00 ET):

- 14 Mar 2027 is the 2027 US DST start; the EU transition is the last Sunday of March (28 Mar 2027 by rule, but the cited source gives no EU 2027 date), so another two-week mismatch window is expected; the engine should use exchange calendars (America/New_York, Europe/Berlin) and never hard-code 15:30 [M].
- Daily-return evidence is consistent across the three sources: there is a small real first-candle directional signal (about 0.10-0.13 R over random direction in NQ, SPX, DAX, FTSE per MQL5), but retail CFD costs consume it. On NQ net is +0.002 R, on DAX -0.038 R. [M]
- Sample size consequence (author's arithmetic, t = 2, sigma = 2.2 R per trade from the project report): detecting a true +0.13 R needs about 1,150 trades, +0.08 R about 3,000, +0.06 R about 5,400, and +0.002 R about 4.8 million; at a net edge near zero the verified net P&L will be statistically indistinguishable from zero for any feasible sample [M].
- Honest implication: for a measurement instrument whose success criterion is a verified net P&L, the likely outcome is "reconciled, correctly accounted, statistically zero or negative"; that is a valid result only if costs, slippage and tax-relevant records are exact. The route choice should therefore optimise measurement quality (fee/spread transparency, statements that reconcile) over profit potential.
- Instrument suitability for a broker-side stop ORB: index CFD (tiny cost in R, no minimums, 24/5, stop held by the broker's platform, gap risk at the open unaddressed by non-guaranteed stops; guaranteed stop premium on Capital.com is 0.02% only when triggered); US stock at IBKR (exchange-routed stop orders, but cost 0.26 R+ at 1,000 USD and unusable at 20 USD); stock CFD (cost 0.5-1.0 R at a 0.3% stop, poor); micro futures (lowest percentage cost and exchange-held stops, but position size and margin do not fit 20-1,000 USD); GPW shares (0% commission at XTB, but narrow universe and unknown spreads) [M].
- Liquidity at the open: the 5-minute window right at the open is when spread and slippage are widest, which is where the MQL5 study's fixed 2.5 point assumption is most likely to be optimistic; the Capital.com off-hours spread snapshot (US100 1.8 pts) is therefore not a safe proxy for in-session ORB entries [L, inference].

- Minimum stop distances for index/stock CFDs (Capital.com, XTB) and for IBKR: not published on the pages fetched; need the platform spec or API instrument-details endpoint.
- No credible ORB study found for Xetra or GPW cash/DAX futures beyond the DAX CFD line in the MQL5 post; no futures-with-commission-only test of the Zarattini rule exists (the MQL5 author says so explicitly).

## 3. Polish tax and reporting (PIT-38, PIT/ZG, CFDs, futures, burden, art. 30b exclusion, tools)

### Takeaway
Gains and losses from shares, CFDs, forex CFDs and futures held at a foreign broker are all reported in one PIT-38 basket under art. 30b (19%), converted per transaction at the NBP average rate of the last business day before each revenue/cost date, with PIT/ZG per source country and no PIT-8C from the foreign broker. The loss carry-forward is NOT a simple "5 years": it is 50% of the loss per year over five years (or a one-off up to PLN 5 million). Art. 30b ust. 4 excludes dispositions made "in the course of business activity"; for a private, automated account that risk looks low but not zero and I found no ruling that addresses algorithmic retail trading. All of this is general information that needs a Polish tax adviser.

### Cited Findings
- PIT-38 covers revenue from disposal of securities and derivative financial instruments and from exercise of rights under them; foreign-currency revenue and costs are converted "according to art. 11a" at the NBP average rate of the last business day preceding the day the revenue was earned or the cost incurred. — [MF brochure to PIT-38 for 2025](https://www.podatki.gov.pl/media/g5ebnm2e/broszura-do-pit-38-za-2025-r.pdf) [H]
- Art. 11a ust. 1-2 text: revenues and costs in foreign currency are converted at the NBP average rate "z ostatniego dnia roboczego poprzedzającego dzień uzyskania przychodu / poniesienia kosztu". — [art. 11a PIT (LexLege)](https://lexlege.pl/ustawa-o-podatku-dochodowym-od-osob-fizycznych/art-11a/) [H for statutory text, secondary host]
- Revenue from derivatives and from exercising rights from derivatives arises "w momencie realizacji tych praw"; revenue from selling securities arises when ownership passes to the buyer. — [MF brochure 2025](https://www.podatki.gov.pl/media/g5ebnm2e/broszura-do-pit-38-za-2025-r.pdf) [H]
- Rate 19% (art. 30b ust. 1 pkt 1 covers securities or derivatives and rights from them; ust. 2 pkt 3 defines derivative income, costs under art. 23 ust. 1 pkt 38a); ust. 3 applies double-tax treaties with a residence certificate; ust. 5a-5b give a credit for foreign tax capped at the proportional share; derivatives and securities are in the same basket. — [art. 30b PIT (LexLege)](https://lexlege.pl/ustawa-o-podatku-dochodowym-od-osob-fizycznych/art-30b/) [H]
- Loss rule (art. 9 ust. 3): reduce income from the same source in the next five consecutive tax years, by no more than 50% of the loss in any year, or once by up to PLN 5,000,000 with the remainder subject to the same 50% annual cap; losses from securities and derivatives are listed together for the PIT-38 loss line. — [MF brochure 2025](https://www.podatki.gov.pl/media/g5ebnm2e/broszura-do-pit-38-za-2025-r.pdf) and [podatki.gov.pl share sale page](http://podatki.gov.pl/pit/rozliczenie-ze-sprzedazy-akcji) [H]. A commercial site claims "max 50% of the loss" and also mentions an "old rule" of 100%, and advises checking with an adviser; it contradicts nothing in the official text but is unreliable. — [Freenance day trading](https://freenance.io/inwestowanie/day-trading-w-polsce-2026-realna-rentownosc-podatki-koszty/) [L]
- FIFO is statutory for securities in art. 24 ust. 10 ("kolejno papierów wartościowych nabytych najwcześniej", applied separately per securities account); art. 30b ust. 7 is the FIFO rule for fund participation units. Several Polish guides cite art. 30b ust. 7 for shares, which is wrong. The FIFO text refers only to "papiery wartościowe", so how lots of CFDs/futures are matched is not settled by this provision. — [art. 24 PIT (LexLege)](https://lexlege.pl/ustawa-o-podatku-dochodowym-od-osob-fizycznych/art-24/); [art. 30b PIT (LexLege)](https://lexlege.pl/ustawa-o-podatku-dochodowym-od-osob-fizycznych/art-30b/); wrong citation example [Freenance PIT-38 broker guide](https://freenance.io/podatki/pit-38-broker-zagraniczny-2026-interactive-brokers-degiro-saxo-jak-rozliczyc) [H for statute, L for guide]
- Foreign brokers (IBKR, DEGIRO, Saxo) do not issue Polish PIT-8C, so revenue and costs are computed from the broker statement; PIT/ZG is filed per source country; deadline 30 April (filing window 15 Feb-30 Apr). — [Freenance guide](https://freenance.io/podatki/pit-38-broker-zagraniczny-2026-interactive-brokers-degiro-saxo-jak-rozliczyc) [L]; [MF brochure on PIT/ZG and deadline](https://www.podatki.gov.pl/media/g5ebnm2e/broszura-do-pit-38-za-2025-r.pdf) [H]; PIT-8C from a Polish brokerage per [podatki.gov.pl share sale](http://podatki.gov.pl/pit/rozliczenie-ze-sprzedazy-akcji) [H]
- CFDs and forex: classified as income from derivative instruments and reported on PIT-38 at 19% (secondary tax-software/portal articles; I did not find a ministry page naming CFDs). — [Pitax: how to settle forex income](https://www.pitax.pl/wiedza/aktualnosci/jak-rozliczyc-dochody-z-forex/) [M]
- Art. 30b ust. 4: "Przepisu ust. 1 nie stosuje się, jeżeli odpłatne zbycie ... papierów wartościowych i pochodnych instrumentów finansowych oraz realizacja praw z nich wynikających ... następuje w wykonywaniu działalności gospodarczej." — [art. 30b ust. 4 (LexLege)](https://lexlege.pl/ustawa-o-podatku-dochodowym-od-osob-fizycznych/art-30b/) [H]
- Art. 5a pkt 6 defines business activity as gainful activity (manufacturing, construction, trade, services, extraction, using things and intangibles) carried on in one's own name regardless of result, in an organised and continuous manner, "z której uzyskane przychody nie są zaliczane do innych przychodów ze źródeł wymienionych w art. 10 ust. 1 pkt 1, 2 i 4-9" (pkt 7 of art. 10 ust. 1 is "kapitały pieniężne i prawa majątkowe"). — [art. 5a PIT (LexLege)](https://lexlege.pl/ustawa-o-podatku-dochodowym-od-osob-fizycznych/art-5a/) and [art. 10 PIT (LexLege)](https://lexlege.pl/ustawa-o-podatku-dochodowym-od-osob-fizycznych/art-10/) [H]
- NSA (hearing 10 Jan 2017; lower court WSA Szczecin I SA/Sz 330/14): income from derivatives (currency forwards, options, CIRS) of a business owner is not business income unless the transactions are the realisation of the business's own subject matter and an independent form of activity; hedging-type use does not qualify. The excerpt does not address trading frequency. — [Podatki.biz summary of the NSA judgment](https://www.podatki.biz/artykuly/nsa-zyski-z-transakcji-walutowych-jako-przychod-z-dzialalnosci_28_34182.htm) [M; secondary summary, case number of the NSA ruling not shown]
- A commercial article says a single trader with PIT-38 "has good chances" of avoiding reclassification but the risk rises with large turnover and treating trading as a main job, the boundary being "fluid"; it cites no ruling or KIS interpretation. — [Freenance day trading](https://freenance.io/inwestowanie/day-trading-w-polsce-2026-realna-rentownosc-podatki-koszty/) [L]
- Tools: kloPIT (free, open source; imports an IBKR CSV activity statement, fetches NBP rates, applies FIFO, fills PIT-38 and PIT/ZG; one user-reported 31.12.2025 bug said to be fixed) [M]; Taxmaster (IBKR, Degiro, XTB, Freedom24) and MyFund (XTB; IBKR via CSV) per vendor-affiliated rankings [L]; no import support found for Capital.com, cTrader or Saxo. — [kloPIT on GitHub](https://github.com/SkeLLLa/klopit); [Freenance tax software ranking](https://freenance.io/rankings/best-tax-software-poland/)
- Dividends (brief): the Poland-US treaty allows up to 15% US withholding on dividends (30% without W-8BEN) and leaves capital gains to residence state with exceptions. — [IRS treaty text](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/poland.pdf) (as already summarised in the [project report](/home/user/-gw-stock-engine/docs/research/2026-10-31-silnik-tradingowy-akcje-usa.md)) [M]

### Inferences
- Burden of tiny trades: at 5-10 trades a day the account generates about 2,500-5,000 statement rows a year, each needing a per-date NBP rate for both the proceeds and the commission line; this is a tool job (kloPIT or a custom ledger export), not a manual spreadsheet. Because revenue and cost are converted on different dates, a flat USD result can still show a small PLN gain or loss [M, from art. 11a].
- Compliance economics: at 20 USD, even an adviser's one-hour consultation exceeds any realistic annual P&L; at 1,000 USD the same holds; therefore the tax work should be bounded by building the ledger export once and ordering a single paid consultation up front [M].
- The 50% per-year loss cap means a losing account recovers its loss slowly: a 100 USD-equivalent loss can offset at most 50% per year against gains in the same basket for five years, with a possible leftover that expires [M, derived from art. 9 ust. 3]. The loss must be declared in the PIT-38 of the year it arises (standard practice; not verified in the brochure text read).
- CFD losses and share gains share one basket; virtual currencies are separate (art. 30b ust. 1a) [H].
- Art. 30b ust. 4 risk: the statute's carve-out is for dispositions "in the course of business activity", and art. 5a pkt 6 itself excludes activity whose revenues fall into the capital-gains source (art. 10 ust. 1 pkt 7); for a private person trading own money in a private brokerage account, PIT-38 treatment looks the base case. Risk factors I would flag to an adviser: very high order count, systematic automation, intent to earn a living, borrowed funds, and any advertising or third-party capital [M, own reading of the statute; no ruling on algorithmic retail trading found]. Whether frequency alone can tip a private investor into "organised and continuous" activity was not resolved by any source I could access.
- Futures at a foreign broker (CME/Eurex): same art. 30b basket; the revenue is recognised on realisation (closing), not on daily mark-to-market, per the brochure's wording; lot-matching for futures and CFD positions is not covered by art. 24 ust. 10 FIFO, so the adviser should confirm the identification method [M-L].

### Gaps
- No ministry page explicitly naming CFDs/forex as art. 30b derivatives; classification rests on secondary portals and the statutory wording of "pochodne instrumenty finansowe".
- No KIS interpretation or NSA ruling on automated/high-frequency retail trading as business activity was found (one portal mentions a 2025-07-15 KIS interpretation on frequent crypto P2P trades, content not verified).
- Whether IBKR Ireland / Capital.com / XTB statements are accepted without further conversion, and how to report swaps, financing and market-data fees as costs (art. 23 ust. 1 pkt 38a): not verified.
- Tool support for Capital.com / cTrader / Saxo statements: not found.

## 4. Regulation and protection (ESMA/KNF CFD rules, compensation, professional reclassification, KNF warnings)

### Takeaway
EU/Polish retail CFD trading is capped at 30:1 for major FX, 20:1 for major indices, 5:1 for shares, with 50% margin close-out, negative balance protection and mandatory loss-percentage warnings, and KNF's 2019 decision applies the same rules (indefinitely) to any provider selling to Polish retail clients. Compensation schemes (Ireland EUR 20,000 at 90%, Poland about EUR 20,100 maximum) are irrelevant to market losses and nearly irrelevant to an account of 20-1,000 USD; KNF is in the middle of an open CFD review (no timetable) after a PLN 20 million fine on XTB, which is a regulatory-change risk for any CFD route.

### Cited Findings
- ESMA product-intervention package (published 2018-03-27; OLD, but still the baseline): leverage limits on opening a retail CFD position of 30:1 (major currency pairs), 20:1 (non-major pairs, gold, major indices), 10:1 (other commodities, non-major equity indices), 5:1 (individual equities and other references), 2:1 (crypto); margin close-out at 50% of required margin per account; negative balance protection per account; ban on incentives; standardised risk warning with the provider's loss percentage. — [ESMA press release](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-agrees-prohibit-binary-options-and-restrict-cfds-protect-retail-investors) [H, OLD]
- KNF decision of 1 Aug 2019 (OLD): unanimous decision introducing leverage limits, close-out protection, negative balance protection, warned materials and ban on bonuses; indefinite duration; applies to investment firms, banks, branches of foreign firms and entities offering cross-border under freedom to provide services. It creates an "experienced retail client" category on written request (for instance 10 opening transactions of at least EUR 50,000 notional per quarter over four quarters, or 50 of at least EUR 10,000, or EUR 2 million total with 40 per quarter, plus a certificate, 50 hours of derivatives training in the last 12 months, or one year of relevant professional experience), for whom the provider may use margin as low as 1% on selected FX pairs and indices (DAX30, S&P 500, NASDAQ 100 etc.) and gold, while other protections stay. — [money.pl report on the KNF decision](https://www.money.pl/gielda/knf-wprowadza-dodatkowe-wymogi-przy-oferowaniu-kontraktow-cfd-6408890710726785a.html) [M, OLD, secondary report]
- KNF fined XTB PLN 20 million (decision dated 2026-03-30) for MiFID II failures in CFD client-knowledge assessment, target-market definition and risk disclosures (conduct Jan 2022-Sep 2023), including a list highlighting high-performing clients; XTB asked for reconsideration so the fine is not final. — [Finance Magnates, 2026-04-13](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/polish-regulator-hits-xtb-with-55m-fine-over-cfd-marketing-rules); [Finance Magnates, 2026-08-18](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/knf-keeps-cfd-review-open-as-xtb-shares-fall-nearly-7/) [M]
- KNF chairman Dariusz Adamski said in May 2026 that access to complex high-risk instruments was too easy; on 17 Aug 2026 KNF said its review of how CFDs are offered (domestic and cross-border, including client knowledge tests) continues, with no timetable or draft rule published. — [Finance Magnates, 2026-08-18](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/knf-keeps-cfd-review-open-as-xtb-shares-fall-nearly-7/) [M]
- Broker disclosures of losing retail accounts: Capital.com 74-89% (per its pages), XTB 74% (per its pages). — [Capital.com US100](https://capital.com/en-eu/markets/indices/us-tech-100-index); [XTB US100](https://www.xtb.com/en/indices/us100) [M, vendor-reported]
- IBKR Ireland (regulated by the Central Bank of Ireland, member of the Irish Investor Compensation Scheme): ICS pays 90% of the loss up to EUR 20,000 per investor, for private individuals, against firm failure, not market loss; client funds are segregated and reconciled daily; IBIE custodies certain securities with US affiliate IBLLC, a SIPC member, giving SIPC cover of up to USD 500,000 (cash sublimit USD 250,000) "to the extent" assets are custodied at IBLLC; futures and options on futures are not covered by SIPC. — [IBKR Ireland client protection](https://interactivebrokers.ie/en/general/security-investor-protection.php) [H]
- Polish brokerage compensation (BFG/KNF scheme): 100% of covered funds up to EUR 3,000 plus 90% of the excess, upper limit the PLN equivalent of EUR 22,000, giving a maximum of about EUR 20,100 (my arithmetic matches an Invezz statement). — [Law Insider copy of the Polish scheme text](https://www.lawinsider.com/pl/contracts/fivZRqNRdHu); [Invezz XTB review](https://invezz.com/pl/recenzje/xtb/) [M-L]
- Professional on request (MiFID II Annex II logic, shown here via UK/other rulebook copies): at least two of three: average of 10 transactions of significant size per quarter over the previous four quarters; financial-instrument portfolio including cash above EUR 500,000; at least one year working in the financial sector in a professional position requiring knowledge of the transactions. The request must be written and the firm assesses it. — [FCA COBS 3.5](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/cobs3/cobs3s5); [Pictet Luxembourg disclosure](https://dev-www.pictet.com/content/dam/www/domifidLUXen.pdf.coredownload.pdf) [M; UK text differs in detail]
- KNF public warnings list (firms for which KNF has filed a criminal complaint): published at knf.gov.pl; I did not retrieve or search it for the shortlisted brokers. — [KNF ostrzeżenia publiczne](https://www.knf.gov.pl/dla_konsumenta/ostrzezenia_publiczne) [URL only, not read]

### Inferences
- Reclassification to professional is neither feasible nor desirable: a 20-1,000 USD portfolio cannot meet the EUR 500,000 test, so two of three criteria would need the 10-trades/quarter test plus a financial-sector job; the owner would also lose the ESMA/KNF leverage caps, close-out, negative balance protection and, at IBKR Ireland, the ICS (which excludes professional clients) [M].
- Negative balance protection is the one retail protection that matters for a bug-prone autonomous engine on leveraged CFDs; it should be a reason to prefer an EU-regulated CFD provider over any off-shore entity, and the engine should still enforce its own hard loss limits because the broker's close-out is a last resort at 50% margin [M].
- The KNF review is a live product-availability risk for the CFD route (and for Polish retail access to XTB-style services) over the project horizon; it argues for keeping the broker interface swappable [M-L].
- Deposits of 20-1,000 USD sit well inside every compensation scheme, so compensation does not discriminate between the routes; custody/segregation and operational failure risk matter more [M].

### Gaps
- Current (2026) ESMA statement on whether the 2018 measures changed: not found. The leverage numbers are 2018 vintage but match the Capital.com and XTB product pages in 2026 (5% index margin, 20% share margin).
- Cyprus ICF (Capital.com) compensation limits: not verified.
- Whether any shortlisted broker (Capital.com, Pepperstone, IC Markets, IG, Admiral) appears on KNF's warning list: not checked.

## 5. Hosting (cheap EU VPS) and making the engine container the first to die

### Takeaway
A separate 4 GB VPS costs about PLN 20 gross/month at OVHcloud, EUR 5.49-5.99 excl. VAT at Hetzner (CX23 shows "currently unavailable"; ARM CAX11 EUR 5.99), and EUR 8.26-9.50 at netcup, which removes any OOM interaction with the real-money bot at the price of roughly 25-30% of a 20 USD account per month. If the crowded VPS is kept, a Docker memory limit with swap equal to memory plus oom_score_adj=1000 confines the engine's damage to its own cgroup and makes it the preferred kill target.

- Contabo and Scaleway pricing pages returned HTTP 403 and were not read.

**Memory-limit and OOM mechanics (primary docs)**
- Docker: "if the kernel detects that there isn't enough memory ... starts killing processes ... Any process is subject to killing, including Docker"; Docker lowers the OOM priority of the daemon but not of containers; do not set extreme negative oom-score-adj. — [Docker resource constraints](https://docs.docker.com/engine/containers/resource_constraints/) [H]
- Docker: `--memory-swap` is the total of memory plus swap and only works with `--memory`; setting `--memory-swap` equal to `--memory` gives the container no swap; if `--memory-swap` is unset and `--memory` is set, the container may use as much swap as memory (300m memory -> 600m total) when the host has swap; some kernels print "WARNING: No swap limit support". — [Docker resource constraints](https://docs.docker.com/engine/containers/resource_constraints/) [H]
- `docker run --oom-score-adj` accepts -1000 to 1000; Compose supports `mem_limit`, `memswap_limit`, `mem_reservation` and `oom_score_adj` (range -1000 to 1000). — [docker container run reference](https://docs.docker.com/reference/cli/docker/container/run/); [Compose services reference](https://docs.docker.com/reference/compose-file/services/) [H]
- Linux: oom_score_adj (-1000 to +1000) is added to the badness score (which grows with memory and swap use); -1000 disables OOM killing for the task and +1000 means always preferred. — [proc_pid_oom_score_adj(5)](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man5/proc_pid_oom_score_adj.5.html) [H]
- cgroup v2: `memory.max` is the hard limit; when usage reaches it and cannot be reduced the OOM killer is invoked in that cgroup; a cgroup-local OOM never kills tasks outside the cgroup; `memory.swap.max` bounds swap use for the cgroup; `memory.oom.group` can make the whole group die together. — [Linux kernel cgroup-v2 documentation](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/cgroup-v2.html) [H]

### Inferences
- A second host gives the strongest isolation (no shared memory or swap) and costs about PLN 20 (OVH, gross) to roughly PLN 30 (Hetzner EUR 5.49 + 0.50 IPv4 excl. VAT with 23% VAT at an assumed EUR/PLN of about 4.25; both the rate and the VAT treatment, German 19% versus Polish 23% for a consumer, are unverified) per month; against a 20 USD account this is about a quarter of the capital each month, so hosting cost is a project cost, not trading cost, and should be presented that way [M-L].
- Latency is not a design constraint: a 1-5 minute bar ORB engine reacts in seconds, so any Frankfurt, Nuremberg or Amsterdam VPS is adequate. No measured round-trip times to IBKR Ireland (Dublin), Capital.com or Eurex endpoints were found; any claim that a given VPS is "closer" is unverified [L].
- Recommended container settings if the crowded host is kept (derived from the quoted docs): `--memory=256m` (or 300m; the engine is 100-200 MB), `--memory-swap=256m` (equal to memory so the engine adds nothing to the host's already 1.1/2 GB used swap), `--memory-reservation` below the limit, `--oom-score-adj=1000` (maximum preference to be killed in a host-wide OOM), `--pids-limit`, and a restart policy that does NOT auto-restart into a loop without the reconciliation start-up path. Equivalent Compose keys: `mem_limit`, `memswap_limit`, `mem_reservation`, `oom_score_adj` [M].
- What this achieves and does not: the cgroup limit means the engine itself hits OOM at its own limit and nothing else is killed; in a genuine host-wide shortage the +1000 adjustment makes the engine the first candidate; it does not prevent swap thrashing slowing the real-money bot before the kernel kills anything, nor shield the bot if the bot itself exceeds RAM. Leave the real-money bot at the default or a moderately negative oom_score_adj (Docker advises against extreme negatives) and verify with `docker inspect` and `/proc/<pid>/oom_score_adj` [M]. If the kernel lacks swap accounting (Docker's "No swap limit support" warning), `--memory-swap` may be ignored on that host [M].
- The 700 MB "free" figure plus swap in use indicates the host is already memory-pressured; a 256-300 MB hard cap for the engine, with SQLite WAL page cache counted inside the cgroup, is a safer budget than the full 700 MB [M-L].

- VAT treatment for a Polish consumer at Hetzner/netcup: not verified.

## 6. Paper-to-live realism per route

### Takeaway
No route offers a paper or demo environment documented to model slippage, spreads and rejections realistically: IBKR says its simulator "cannot exactly replicate live markets", market-maker CFD brokers (XTB states it is a market maker) price demo against their own feed, and I found no regulator study quantifying demo-versus-live gaps. Paper should be treated as a plumbing and state-machine test only; cost realism requires micro-live fills with an independent reference feed.

### Cited Findings
- IBKR knowledge base (last updated 2024-11-11, OLD): a paper account is "a simulated account trading in a simulated market"; IBKR simulates the exchange role; "there is no way to exactly replicate live markets ... no simulator could be perfect"; paper executions may not correspond to the market at the time. — [IBKR KB article 252](https://ibkb.interactivebrokers.com/article/252) [H, OLD]
- IBKR education material lists market impact, price slippage, microstructure, price improvement, regulatory fees and dividends as items paper trading does not account for (read via search summary); IBKR handles market orders as marketable limit or "Market With Protection" orders. — [IBKR Campus: from back-test to paper trading](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/ibkr-quant-news/from-back-test-to-paper-trading/); [IBKR simulated market orders](https://www.interactivebrokers.com.au/en/trading/simulated-market-orders.php) [M]
- Forum reports of unrealistic IBKR paper fills in volatile moments exist but are anecdotal. — [NexusFi thread](https://nexusfi.com/traders-hideout/56984-bad-fills-ninjatrader-8-ib-interactivebrokers-2.html) [L]
- CFD demo accounts: sources disagree (a broker forum reply says demo spreads equal live spreads; an explainer says demo spreads stay constant while live widens); no regulator study or published demo-versus-live statistic was found. — [DailyForex demo vs real](https://www.dailyforex.com:443/forex-articles/2020/11/differences-demo-and-real/153282); [InvestorTrip IC Markets demo note](https://www.investortrip.com/reviews/ic-markets/demo-account) [L]
- XTB states it operates as a market maker (the counterparty to its CFD clients); KNF's 2026 fine cites misleading CFD risk communication. — [XTB swap table](https://xtb.scdn5.secure.raxcdn.com/file/0090/17/5c4bb8e0-fb7f-4218-ba25-387ef6bb9bae/swaps-pl.pdf); [Finance Magnates, 2026-04-13](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/polish-regulator-hits-xtb-with-55m-fine-over-cfd-marketing-rules) [M]
- Capital.com's pages state the cost of a trade is the spread and show an in-product demo account; no statement about demo spread fidelity was found. — [Capital.com US100](https://capital.com/en-eu/markets/indices/us-tech-100-index) [M]

### Inferences
- IBKR US stocks / futures: IBKR paper validates the order state machine, API behaviours and commission mechanics (including minimum-fee effects) but not slippage, queue position or rejection modes; paper results are an upper bound on gross edge [M].
- CFDs (Capital.com, XTB and similar): the "slippage" is the broker's pricing decision, so a demo run cannot validate it; the only honest measurement is micro-live with the engine logging the quote at decision time, the executed price, and an independent index reference feed to compute implied spread and slippage per trade; compare in-session opening-minute spreads against the off-hours values quoted above [M].
- Micro-live is cheapest on index/FX CFDs (no minimum fees, positions of cents) and most expensive in relative terms on IBKR US stocks at 20 USD (2% per round trip); a measurement project that wants cost realism per route should therefore pick the route for measurement efficiency, not for profit [M].
- Known demo-to-live gaps to build into the cost model as haircuts: spread widening at the open, stop-order gap slippage, partial fills and rejections on small stock orders, and, if the account is funded in PLN, the cost of converting the deposit: IBKR's USD 2.00 manual FX minimum is 10% of a 20 USD conversion (0.2% of 1,000 USD), so use auto-conversion (0.03%) or fund directly in USD [M].
